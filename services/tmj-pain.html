Common questions

Understand what
your jaw is telling you.

The temporomandibular joints connect your jaw to your skull. A clinician can assess your symptoms and discuss appropriate next steps.

What can jaw discomfort feel like?

Some people notice soreness in the jaw or face, clicking, tenderness when chewing, limited movement, ear-area discomfort, or headaches near the temples.

What may contribute to TMJ symptoms?

Jaw symptoms may have several contributing factors, including muscle tension, clenching or grinding, injury, or joint conditions. An evaluation helps consider what may be involved.

How is it evaluated?

Your dentist may ask about your symptoms and examine jaw movement and tenderness. Depending on your situation, they may discuss imaging or a referral.

What treatment might be discussed?

Options depend on the cause and severity. Your care team may discuss habits that reduce strain, a custom night guard, or other care if appropriate.

Can I try anything at home?

Some people find temporary comfort with a warm compress, gentle jaw rest, and softer foods. Check with a clinician for advice that fits your health and symptoms.

When should I seek care?

Arrange an evaluation if pain persists, worsens, or makes it difficult to open or close your mouth. Sudden severe symptoms or an injury need prompt attention.
